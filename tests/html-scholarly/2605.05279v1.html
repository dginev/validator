Quasi sdf-absorbing ideals in commutative rings

Violeta Leoreanu-Fotea∗ Faculty of Mathematics, Al.I. Cuza University, Bd. Carol I, No. 11, 700506 Iaşi, Romania. foteavioleta@gmail.com , Ece Yetkin Celikel Department of Software Engineering, Hasan Kalyoncu University, Gaziantep, Türkiye ece.celikel@hku.edu.tr, yetkinece@gmail.com , Tarik Arabaci Department of Basic Science, Faculty of Engineering and Architecture, Istanbul Gelişim University, Istanbul, Türkiye tarabaci@gelisim.edu.tr and Unsal Tekir Department of Mathematics, Marmara University, Istanbul, Türkiye utekir@marmara.edu.tr
Abstract.

This paper introduces and studies quasi sdf-absorbing ideals as a generalization of sdf-absorbing ideals. We investigate the stability of this property under various constructions, including localization, surjective images, Nagata idealizations, and amalgamations. We establish conditions under which the radical of such ideals is prime and discuss a specific class of rings where quasi sdf-absorption implies the sdf-absorbing primary property. The study concludes with a classification of these ideals in ℤ\mathbb{Z} and examples distinguishing them from related ideal classes.

Key words and phrases:
Square-difference factor absorbing ideal, sdf-absorbing primary ideal, quasi-sdf-absorbing ideal

1. Introduction

The study of ideal-theoretic generalizations of prime and primary ideals has attracted considerable attention in recent years. Such generalizations often arise by weakening the defining conditions of classical notions while preserving important structural properties, a framework thoroughly discussed in classical texts like [3] and [13]. A notable direction in this area was initiated by A. Badawi [4], who introduced the concept of 22-absorbing ideals, extending the classical notion of prime ideals. This work was further expanded to the primary case in [5], where 22-absorbing primary ideals were characterized.

Throughout this paper, all rings are assumed to be commutative with identity. Motivated by similar ideas, Anderson, Badawi, and Coykendall [2] recently introduced the class of square-difference factor absorbing ideals (briefly, sdf-absorbing ideals) of rings.. An ideal II of a ring RR is called sdf-absorbing if for all non-zero a,b∈Ra,b\in R, a2−b2∈Ia^{2}-b^{2}\in I implies a−b∈I​or​a+b∈I.a-b\in I\;\text{or}\;a+b\in I. A natural extension of this notion, which involves the radical of the ideal, was proposed by Khashan, Yetkin Celikel, and Tekir [11]. They defined an ideal II of a ring RR to be sdf-absorbing primary if for all a,b∈Ra,b\in R, a2−b2∈Ia^{2}-b^{2}\in I implies a+b∈I​or​a−b∈I.a+b\in\sqrt{I}\;\text{or}\;a-b\in I. A fundamental structural result established in [11] states that if II is an sdf-absorbing primary ideal, then its radical I\sqrt{I} is necessarily sdf-absorbing. However, the converse of this implication does not hold in general (See Example 2.1 (i)).

Inspired by this connection, the present paper is devoted to the study of quasi sdf-absorbing ideals, defined as those ideals II whose radicals I\sqrt{I} are sdf-absorbing. This class provides a flexible framework for analyzing the ”absorbing” nature of an ideal in rings that are not necessarily reduced. We investigate the behavior of these ideals under various constructions, including the amalgamation extension, a structure recently surveyed in [7]. Specifically, we provide a complete characterization of quasi sdf-absorbing ideals in the ring of integers ℤ\mathbb{Z} and analyze their transfer properties in idealizations and amalgamated duplications. Furthermore, we identify and explore a particular class of rings where the quasi sdf-absorbing property and the sdf-absorbing primary property coincide, examining how this relationship is preserved under fundamental ring-theoretic operations.

2. Properties of Quasi sdf-absorbing ideals

In 1946, Fuchs introduced the concept of quasi-primary ideals. According to [8] a proper ideal is said to be quasi-primary if its radical is prime. More generally, in 1995, Jayaram and Johnson [10] studied this notion in a more general lattice-theoretic framework. Motivated by these concepts, we introduce in this work a natural generalization, namely the class of quasi sdf-absorbing ideals. The main result of this study is presented below.

Definition 2.1.

Let RR be a ring and II a proper ideal of R.R. We say that II is quasi sdf-absorbing if its radical I\sqrt{I} is a sdf-absorbing ideal of R.R.

It follows immediately that every quasi-primary and sdf-absorbing ideals are quasi sdf-absorbing. Nevertheless, the converse implication does not hold in general, as illustrated by the following example.

Example 2.1.
  1. (1)

    Consider the ideal I=4​qm​ℤI=4q^{m}\mathbb{Z} of ℤ\mathbb{Z}, where qq is an odd prime and m≥1m\geq 1. Then II is a quasi sdf-absorbing ideal, but it is not an sdf-absorbing primary ideal. Indeed, I=2​q​ℤ,\sqrt{I}=2q\mathbb{Z}, which is an sdf-absorbing ideal, by [2, Example 2.8 (a)], therefore, II is quasi sdf-absorbing. However, II is not an sdf-absorbing primary ideal.   2. (2)

    Let KK be a field, let qq be an odd prime, and m≥1m\geq 1. Consider the ideal I=4​qm​K​[x1,x2,…,xn]I=4q^{m}K[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}] of K​[x1,x2,…,xn].K[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}].   3. (3)

    In any ring RR with characteristic 22 (in particular, if RR is a Boolean ring), every proper ideal of RR is quasi sdf-absorbing. Indeed, let II be a proper ideal of RR with c​h​a​r​(R)=2char(R)=2. Suppose that a2−b2∈Ia^{2}-b^{2}\in\sqrt{I}, for some 0≠a,b∈R.0\neq a,b\in R. Next, we verify that if 22 is a unit element in a ring, then the quasi sdf-absorbing and quasi primary ideals coincide.

Proposition 2.1.

Let RR be a ring such that 22 is a unit element. Then, a proper ideal of RR is quasi sdf-absorbing if and only if it is quasi primary.

Proof.

Let II be a quasi sdf-absorbing ideal of RR and a,b∈Ra,b\in R such that a​b∈Iab\in\sqrt{I}. Then (a+b)2−(a−b)2=4​a​b∈I(a+b)^{2}-(a-b)^{2}=4ab\in\sqrt{I}. Now assume that a=ba=b (respectively, a=−ba=-b). Then, we have a2∈Ia^{2}\in\sqrt{I} (respectively, −a2∈I-a^{2}\in\sqrt{I}) which implies a∈Ia\in\sqrt{I}. Thus, I\sqrt{I} is prime, and so II is a quasi primary ideal of R.R. The converse part is straightforward. ∎

The following observation will be useful in the sequel, as it gives another equivalent form of the definition.

Remark 2.1.

Let II be a proper ideal of a ring R.R. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

  1. (1)

    II is a quasi sdf-absorbing ideal of R.R.

  2. (2)

    For 0≠a,b∈R0\neq a,b\in R such that a2−b2∈Ia^{2}-b^{2}\in\sqrt{I}, we have a−b∈Ia-b\in\sqrt{I} or a+b∈Ia+b\in\sqrt{I}.

  3. (3)

    For a,b∈Ra,b\in R such that a2−b2∈Ia^{2}-b^{2}\in\sqrt{I}, we have a−b∈Ia-b\in\sqrt{I} or a+b∈Ia+b\in\sqrt{I}.

Suppose a2−b2∈Ia^{2}-b^{2}\in\sqrt{I} for some a,b∈R.a,b\in R. If a,ba,b are nonzero, we are done by (2). Without loss of generality, assume that b=0.b=0. 3. Behavior of Quasi sdf-absorbing ideals under Ring Constructions

In this section, we examine the behavior of quasi sdf-absorbing ideals with respect to several fundamental ring constructions, including ring homomorphisms, localizations, quotient rings, cartesian products, idealizations, and amalgamated rings.

4. On the class of rings satisfying condition (∗)(\ast)

In this section, we characterize the class of rings in which the distinction between the quasi sdf-absorbing property and the sdf-absorbing primary property vanishes. We define this class as follows:

Definition 4.1.

A ring RR is said to satisfy condition (∗)(\ast) if every quasi sdf-absorbing ideal of RR is an sdf-absorbing primary ideal.

For a ring RR satisfying (∗)(\ast), the structural relationship between an ideal and its radical becomes particularly clear: for any proper ideal II of RR, the following statements are equivalent:

  1. (1)

    II is an sdf-absorbing primary ideal;

  2. (2)

    I\sqrt{I} is an sdf-absorbing ideal.

We list below several important classes of rings that satisfy condition (∗).(\ast).

Example 4.1.
  1. (1)

    Von Neumann regular rings: In these rings, every ideal is semiprime (I=II=\sqrt{I}). Thus, any quasi sdf-absorbing ideal is its own radical and sdf-absorbing, making (∗)(\ast) trivial.

  2. (2)

    Rings of characteristic 2: In such rings, every proper ideal of RR is sdf-absorbing primary.

  3. (3)

    If 22 is invertible, the notion of an sdf-absorbing ideal coincides with that of a prime ideal and an sdf-absorbing primary ideal is exactly a primary ideal. Consequently, in this setting, Condition (∗)(\ast) reflects the classical property that ideals with prime radicals are primary.

Theorem 4.1.

Let RR be a 0-dimensional Noetherian ring such that 2∈U​(R)2\in U(R). Then RR satisfies the condition (∗)(\ast).

Proof.

Let II be a quasi sdf-absorbing ideal of RR. By definition, L=IL=\sqrt{I} is an sdf-absorbing ideal. Since 2∈U​(R)2\in U(R), the sdf-absorbing property of LL is equivalent to the condition that LL is a prime ideal. Thus, I=𝔭\sqrt{I}=\mathfrak{p} for some 𝔭∈Spec​(R)\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Spec}(R). In a 0-dimensional Noetherian ring, every prime ideal is a maximal ideal. Therefore, 𝔭=𝔪\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m} for some 𝔪∈Max​(R)\mathfrak{m}\in\text{Max}(R). We utilize the following result, see [3, Proposition 4.2]: In a Noetherian ring if QQ is an ideal such that Q\sqrt{Q} is a maximal ideal, then QQ is primary. Since I=𝔪\sqrt{I}=\mathfrak{m} is maximal, it follows directly from the above characterization that II is an 𝔪\mathfrak{m}-primary ideal. Hence II is an sdf-absorbing primary ideal and Condition (∗)(\ast) holds. ∎

Proposition 4.1.

Let RR satisfy (∗)(\ast), let I,JI,J be ideals of RR, I=P\sqrt{I}=P, such that I⊆J⊆PI\subseteq J\subseteq P. If II is PP-sdf-absorbing primary, then JJ is also PP-sdf-absorbing primary.

Proof.

By the monotonicity of the radical operator, the inclusion I⊆J⊆PI\subseteq J\subseteq P implies P⊆J⊆P=PP\subseteq\sqrt{J}\subseteq\sqrt{P}=P, hence J=P\sqrt{J}=P. Since II is PP-sdf-absorbing primary, by [11, Theorem 1] PP is sdf-absorbing, then JJ is a quasi sdf-absorbing ideal. Under the assumption that RR satisfies condition (∗)(\ast), every quasi sdf-absorbing ideal is sdf-absorbing primary. Thus, JJ is PP-sdf-absorbing primary. ∎

Proposition 4.2.

Let RR satisfy condition (∗)(\ast) and let {Qi}i=1n\{Q_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n} be a finite family of PP-sdf-absorbing primary ideals. Then Q=⋂i=1nQiQ=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}Q_{i} is a PP-sdf-absorbing primary ideal.

Proof.

The radical of a finite intersection is the intersection of the radicals: Q=⋂i=1nQi=⋂i=1nQi=P.\sqrt{Q}=\sqrt{\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}Q_{i}}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{Q_{i}}=P. Since PP is an sdf-absorbing ideal, QQ is quasi sdf-absorbing by definition. By condition (∗)(\ast), QQ is an sdf-absorbing primary ideal with radical PP. ∎

Proposition 4.3.

Let RR and R′R^{\prime} be rings and f:R→R′f:R\rightarrow R^{\prime} be a surjective ring homomorphism. If RR satisfies condition (∗)(\ast), then R′R^{\prime} also satisfies condition (∗)(\ast).

Let Q′Q^{\prime} be a quasi sdf-absorbing ideal in R′R^{\prime}. To satisfy condition (∗)(\ast), we must show that Q′Q^{\prime} is an sdf-absorbing primary ideal. Since ff is surjective, we can consider the preimage Q=f−1​(Q′)Q=f^{-1}(Q^{\prime}), which is an ideal of RR containing ker⁡(f)\ker(f). First, we observe that Q=f−1​(Q′)\sqrt{Q}=f^{-1}(\sqrt{Q^{\prime}}). Since Q′Q^{\prime} is quasi sdf-absorbing, its radical Q′\sqrt{Q^{\prime}} is an sdf-absorbing ideal in R′R^{\prime}. It is a known property that the preimage of an sdf-absorbing ideal under a surjective homomorphism is also sdf-absorbing. Thus, Q\sqrt{Q} is sdf-absorbing in RR, which by definition makes QQ a quasi sdf-absorbing ideal in RR. Since RR satisfies condition (∗)(\ast), it follows that QQ is an sdf-absorbing primary ideal. Finally, since ff is surjective and ker⁡(f)⊆Q\ker(f)\subseteq Q, the image f​(Q)=Q′f(Q)=Q^{\prime} inherits the sdf-absorbing primary property, by [11, Proposition 4 (2)]. Corollary 4.1.

Let RR be a ring satisfying condition (∗)(\ast). For any ideal JJ of RR, the quotient ring R/JR/J also satisfies condition (∗)(\ast).

Proof.

Consider the canonical projection π:R→R/J\pi:R\rightarrow R/J, which is a surjective ring homomorphism. Since RR satisfies condition (∗)(\ast), it follows directly from the previous Proposition that its surjective image, R/JR/J, must also satisfy condition (∗)(\ast). ∎
